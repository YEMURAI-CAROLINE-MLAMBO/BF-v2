# Provisional Patent Application

**Title:** Spatial Light Computing Platform with Atmospheric Volumetric Reconstruction (Step-Out-of-Screen Engine)

**Lead Inventor:** Yemurai Caroline Mlambo

**Date of Disclosure:** June 14, 2024

---

## 1. ABSTRACT
A mobile computing platform (BendingForce v2) that utilizes a multi-layered optical stack and specialized processing units to reconstruct three-dimensional light fields in the atmospheric volume above a display surface. The system treats the air as an uninterrupted canvas, using high-intensity light as a conductor to create "Step-Out-of-Screen" holographic imagery (2cm - 30cm height) visible to the naked eye. The platform includes an AI-managed power distribution network and a zero-copy memory architecture to enable real-time spatial synthesis with sub-10ms latency.

## 2. BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION
Traditional spatial computing is limited by the requirement of head-mounted displays or specialized 2D screens that cannot convey true depth. Existing solutions often feel "unnatural" as they do not occupy the physical space of the user. There is a need for a portable, ruggedized device that can project interactive 3D content directly into the air, bridging the gap between digital data and physical reality.

## 3. SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION
The present invention, developed by Yemurai Caroline Mlambo, relates to a device that utilizes a **Laminated Optical Sandwich (LOS)** and a **Holographic Synthesis Unit (HSU)** to project a volumetric light field. The invention is characterized by:
1.  **Air-as-Canvas Technology:** Utilizing the atmosphere above the device as the primary display medium.
2.  **Laminated Optical Sandwich (LOS):** A 6-layer stack comprising Micro-LEDs, Micro-Lens Arrays, Liquid Crystal Switching, Diffractive Films, Micro-Prism Lattices, and Fluorescent Amplification.
3.  **Intelligent Power Distribution Network (IPDN):** An NPU-driven system that manages high-current pulses for spatial rendering.
4.  **Unified Spatial Memory:** A zero-copy architecture for low-latency data transfer between the SoC and the optical engine.

## 4. DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### 4.1 Atmospheric Volumetric Reconstruction
The system operates on the principle that air can serve as a conductor for structured light patterns. By precisely modulating the phase and angle of photons, the device reconstructs "Crystal Lattice" points in space. These points combine to form solid-appearing 3D objects that the user can interact with directly in the air.

### 4.2 The 6-Layer Laminated Optical Sandwich (LOS)
The light path is strictly controlled through the following layers:
*   **Layer 1 (Emitter):** High-PPI GaN-on-Si Micro-LED array.
*   **Layer 2 (Collimation):** Nano-imprinted Micro-Lens Array.
*   **Layer 3 (Mode Toggle):** Liquid Crystal Switch for 2D/3D transition.
*   **Layer 4 (Phase):** Diffractive Holographic Film with Surface Relief Gratings.
*   **Layer 5 (Parallax):** High-index Micro-Prism Optical Lattice.
*   **Layer 6 (Emission):** Focused Adaptive Lens / Fluorescent Amplification Layer (FAL) for spectral upconversion.

### 4.3 Computational and Energy Framework
The **Holographic Synthesis Unit (HSU)** performs real-time interference pattern calculations, while the **Snapdragon-based IPDN** ensures that the high energy demands of the LOS are met by shunting power from solid-state batteries and supercapacitor arrays during rendering bursts.

## 5. CLAIMS (Conceptual)
1.  A system for spatial light computing that utilizes the atmosphere above a device as an uninterrupted canvas for 3D imagery.
2.  The system of claim 1, comprising a 6-layer laminated optical stack configured to reconstruct light fields in a volume of 2cm to 30cm above the surface.
3.  The system of claim 1, further comprising a specialized co-processor (HSU) and a zero-copy memory pool for sub-10ms spatial frame synthesis.
4.  The system of claim 1, incorporating an AI-managed power network (IPDN) that shunts energy to the optical stack based on predicted rendering loads.
5.  A method for creating a "Step-Out-of-Screen" effect by using the air as a conductor for phase-modulated light wavefronts, as described by Lead Inventor Yemurai Caroline Mlambo.
